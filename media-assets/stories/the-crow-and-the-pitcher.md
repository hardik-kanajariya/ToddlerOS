# The Crow and the Pitcher
*A Classic Aesop's Fable*

On a hot summer day, a thirsty black crow flew across dry fields searching for water to drink.

"I need just a few drops of fresh water," sighed the crow, feeling very weary.

In the corner of a farmhouse garden, the crow spotted a tall clay pitcher. He flew down eagerly and looked inside.

There was water at the bottom of the pitcher! But the water level was low, and the pitcher had a narrow neck. No matter how hard the crow stretched his beak, he could not reach the cool water.

The crow thought for a moment. He looked around the garden and noticed a pile of smooth little pebbles lying near the path.

"Aha!" said the clever crow.

The crow picked up one pebble in his beak: *plop!* He dropped it into the pitcher.
He picked up a second pebble: *plop!*
A third pebble: *plop!*

With every pebble that dropped to the bottom, the water rose a little higher.
Ten pebbles... twenty pebbles... thirty pebbles!

At last, the sparkling water reached the very top of the pitcher rim. The happy crow drank his fill of sweet, cool water.

**Little by little does the trick!**
